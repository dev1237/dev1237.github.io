01 / origin

Who's on the other end of the wire.

I went to school in Bahrain and came to SVNIT Surat for electronics. Somewhere between circuits and signals I got pulled one layer up, into the network. Not the apps. The packets.

The problems I like have their answer sitting in a header field nobody checked. A TTL that's a few hops short. An IP-ID counter that gives two machines away. A reset packet that claims to come from a server that never sent it.

So far that's meant finding holes in my own college's servers (and being allowed to keep looking), keeping a department's SSH box alive for 300 people at once, and spending a summer at IIT Bombay chasing a national censor through a country's internet until I could point at where it sits.

Next stop: back to Bahrain this winter, on infrastructure at Gulf Business Machines.

Trust Lab, IIT BombayMay – Jul 2026 certificate ↗ report ↗
step 1

Build a censor first.

Before measuring a real one, I built my own: a 3-VM testbed with a Linux NFQUEUE middlebox in the path, censoring four protocols four different ways.

Sweep the whole country.

A nation-wide measurement of the UAE: yarrp to find paths that were being tampered with, ZMap to probe them, then confirmation across four protocols.

Result: the DPI was stateless in the measured set.

step 3

Find where it sits.

The resets were source-spoofed to look like they came from the server. TTL arithmetic still gave away how far away the injector really was.

Off-path vs. in-path classification, then an IP-ID side channel: two co-located injectors on the Etisalat (AS8966) backbone.

04 / builds

Things I broke, and things that won't break.

aug 2025 · svnit electronics dept

Highly-available SSH gateway

150+ students on one SSH server meant crashes. I built a two-node active/active gateway on RHEL.

  • Nginx stream (L4) round-robin over TCP/22 with passive health checks. Verified an exact 4:4 split across 8 sessions.
  • keepalived/VRRP on both nodes behind a floating virtual IP, so the load balancer isn't a single point of failure either.
  • Zero crashes since, capacity doubled to 300+ sessions, failover in under 4 s with no dropped sessions. Tested with a graceful shutdown and a forced kill.
